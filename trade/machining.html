Machining (Lathe & Milling)

Make and repair precise machine parts like shafts, bushings and gears on lathe and milling machines.

Quick answer: to learn machining (lathe & milling) in Nigeria, train for about 2–3 years under a master or at a skills centre. Learn to read a vernier caliper accurately, then visit a machine shop and ask to watch a shaft being turned.

Your first step

Learn to read a vernier caliper accurately, then visit a machine shop and ask to watch a shaft being turned.

Summary of the work

Make and repair precise machine parts like shafts, bushings and gears on lathe and milling machines.

Skills training

  • Lathe operation
  • Milling and drilling
  • Precision measuring (vernier, micrometer)
  • Reading engineering drawings
  • Machine maintenance

Training path, step by step

  1. First stepLearn to read a vernier caliper accurately, then visit a machine shop and ask to watch a shaft being turned.
  2. Learn machine safety and measuring tools
  3. Turn simple parts on a lathe
  4. Mill and drill to drawings
  5. Make spare parts with supervision
  6. Sit a trade test (Grade III)
  7. Work in industry or open a machine shop

Certification

Trade test (Grade III, II, I); NABTEB Modular Trade Certificate; welding codes for industrial jobs.

Questions people ask

How long does it take to learn machining (lathe & milling) in Nigeria?

Training usually takes 2–3 years. The path: Learn machine safety and measuring tools, then Turn simple parts on a lathe, then Mill and drill to drawings, then Make spare parts with supervision, then Sit a trade test (Grade III), then Work in industry or open a machine shop.

How do I start learning machining (lathe & milling)?

Learn to read a vernier caliper accurately, then visit a machine shop and ask to watch a shaft being turned.

What skills will I learn in machining (lathe & milling)?

Lathe operation, Milling and drilling, Precision measuring (vernier, micrometer), Reading engineering drawings, Machine maintenance.

What certificate do I need for machining (lathe & milling)?

Trade test (Grade III, II, I); NABTEB Modular Trade Certificate; welding codes for industrial jobs.

How much does it cost to start machining (lathe & milling)?

High start-up cost compared with other trades. We do not list exact prices because they change often: ask two or three masters, compare, and agree the training fee in writing before you start.

Where can I learn machining (lathe & milling) in Nigeria?

Most people learn under a master (oga) in a workshop, at a state or church/mosque skills acquisition centre, or at a government technical college. National programmes such as the ITF Skill-Up Artisans (SUPA) programme and the federal TVET initiative also train and certify people; confirm details on the official .gov.ng website.
