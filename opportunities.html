Opportunities

Programmes, certificates and local help for apprentices in all 36 states and the FCT.

National

Programmes & certificates

Check before you apply. Intakes, stipends and rules change often. Confirm details on the official website (addresses end in .gov.ng). Government programmes do not ask you to pay agents to apply. Information last checked: October 2026.

Industrial Training Fund (ITF)

Skill-Up Artisans (SUPA)

What it is
A national programme that screens, trains and certifies working artisans. The 2026 phase targets about 200,000 artisans.
What you get
Training, local and international certification, tools, business support, and exit routes into jobs, overseas work or business incubation.
Who can apply
Practising artisans. Applicants register online and attend screening in their senatorial district.
Official website

Federal Ministry of Education

TVET Initiative

What it is
Free technical and vocational training in about 28 priority trades at approved centres across the country. Launched in 2025.
What you get
Practical training (about 90% hands-on) for 6 to 12 months, with a monthly stipend and post-training starter support.
Who can apply
Young people who apply through the national portal and are verified with their NIN.
Official website

Federal Ministry of Communications, Innovation & Digital Economy with NITDA

3 Million Technical Talent (3MTT)

What it is
Free training in tech skills such as software development, data analysis, cybersecurity, UI/UX and animation.
What you get
Mostly online training with in-person sessions, an average of three months per course, and placement support.
Who can apply
Nigerians who pass the entry assessment. There is no application fee.
Official website

National Directorate of Employment (NDE)

Vocational skills & open apprenticeship

What it is
Long-running apprenticeship and skills schemes that place young people with master craftsmen and training centres.
What you get
Training in many trades, and in some schemes resettlement support after graduation.
Who can apply
Unemployed young people. Contact the NDE office in your state for current intakes.
Official website

Federal Ministry of Labour and state examinations boards

Trade Test (Grade III, II, I)

What it is
The official test that certifies your skill in a trade. Grade III is the entry level; Grade I is the highest.
What you get
A recognised certificate that helps you win contracts, jobs and government work.
Who can apply
Artisans and apprentices. Register at an accredited trade test centre or your state examinations board.
Official website

NABTEB

Modular Trade Certificate (MTC)

What it is
Certificates for vocational skills that can be earned module by module.
What you get
A national certificate for each trade module you pass.
Who can apply
Learners in technical colleges, skills centres and apprenticeships.
Official website

National Board for Technical Education (NBTE)

Nigerian Skills Qualification (NSQ)

What it is
A national framework that certifies practical skills by level, through approved centres.
What you get
A level-based qualification (Level 1 upwards) recognised by employers.
Who can apply
Learners and working artisans assessed at approved NSQ centres.
Official website

Traditional routes

Learning under a master

  • Master apprenticeship. Most Nigerian artisans learn under a master (oga) in a workshop, then receive their “freedom” when they are ready to work alone.
  • Igba Boi (Igbo apprenticeship system). An apprentice serves a business owner for an agreed period and is then set up with capital or stock to start their own business.
  • Skills acquisition centres. State and local governments, churches, mosques and NGOs run centres with short practical courses.
  • Technical colleges. Government technical colleges teach trades and prepare students for NABTEB exams.

Apprenticeship agreement checklist

Agree these in writing before you start, with a parent or guarantor present.

  • Names of the master, the apprentice and a guarantor
  • Start date and length of training
  • Training fee, how it is paid, and what happens if training ends early
  • Working days and hours, and any feeding or transport support
  • When you will sit a trade test or certificate exam
  • What the freedom ceremony includes, and whether tools are given
  • Signatures of everyone, with a copy for each person
